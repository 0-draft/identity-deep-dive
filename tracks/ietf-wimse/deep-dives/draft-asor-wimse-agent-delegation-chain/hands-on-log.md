## Steps

1. Put the -03 text and the 36 vector files in a local directory `attenu-guard-v0.22.0-draft02/`, outside the repository.
2. Score the vectors: `python3 -I vectors/verify_vectors.py <dir>/attenu-guard-v0.22.0-draft02 > vectors/verify_vectors.log`. The script implements the parse rules of Sections 3, 4, 4.1, 4.2 and 4.4, steps 1 to 5 of Section 6, and the audience part of step 8. It then runs its self-test: the unmodified `valid_chain.json` plus 12 mutations of it, each expected to give a named outcome.
3. Lab 1: `python3 -I vectors/three_hop.py > vectors/three_hop.log`. It mints a three-token chain (planner, researcher, formatter) with alg `Ed25519` from fixed seeds and runs steps 1 to 5 only.
4. Lab 2: `python3 -I vectors/overspend.py > vectors/overspend.log`. It mints root max 100 with two children at 60, verifies both chains, then models two gateways, a shared ledger and a single-writer allocator in-process (threads stand in for the two gateways).
5. Lab 3: fetch the niyikiza text with `gh api repos/tenuo-ai/tenuo/contents/ietf/draft-niyikiza-oauth-attenuating-agent-tokens-02.txt -H "Accept: application/vnd.github.raw"`, then `python3 -I vectors/depth_rules.py > vectors/depth_rules.log`. It implements only the depth checks of niyikiza-02 Section 8 (steps 3c, 3i, 4b4, 4d to 4g, 4m and 5, with MAX_DELEGATION_DEPTH 8) and the asor depth rules, and also runs each case through the full asor verifier on a minted chain.
6. Lab 4: `python3 -I vectors/full_verify.py <dir>/attenu-guard-v0.22.0-draft02 > vectors/full_verify.log`. It mints a three-token Ed25519 chain whose leaf carries all eight constraint types, runs steps 1 to 5 through `verify_vectors.py`, then step 6 (a DPoP proof checked per RFC 9449 Section 4.3 and bound to DT_2's `cnf.jkt` per RFC 9449 Section 6.1 and RFC 7638), step 7 (a cached status list with a time to live) and step 8 (audience, scope, per-action and cumulative constraints). Running totals come from a dict passed beside the request, never from the action's parameters. It then runs the action part of step 8 on `valid_unknown_constraint_identical.json`. The RFC 9449 checks follow the RFC text (Sections 4.3, 6.1 and 11.1, checked on 18 November 2026); the status list draft was not fetched.
7. Lab 4 self-test: `python3 -I vectors/full_verify.py --break <dir>/attenu-guard-v0.22.0-draft02 > vectors/full_verify.break.log` adds one case that expects accept for max_rows 101. It is reported as FAIL and the run exits 1.
8. `python3 -m compileall -q vectors/*.py`, and each Lab 4 run twice with `cmp` on the two outputs.

## Failure Modes

- Checking scopes with a regex that ends in `$`: `crm.read\n` then passes as a literal under `crm.*`. Use a full match (`reject_newline_scope.json`).
- Python's `json` accepts NaN and Infinity and keeps the last of two duplicate members by default. Without hooks the verifier never reports non_finite or duplicate_member.
- Comparing derived lifetimes at step 4 reports not_narrower for `reject_child_exp_exceeds_parent.json`; rule 4 belongs to step 5 (expired).
- Using the -02 time rule (now <= exp) accepts a chain at now == exp.
- `python3 -I` drops the script's directory from `sys.path`, so `three_hop.py`, `overspend.py` and `depth_rules.py` load `verify_vectors.py` by path.
- Lab 2 is an in-process model: threads with a forced interleaving, not two real gateways on a network.
- Lab 4: comparing htu with the full request URI fails every request that carries a query; RFC 9449 Section 4.3 compares it without query and fragment.
- Lab 4: reading a cumulative total from the action's own parameters lets the request state its own spend. Section 4.2 and step 8 keep the total on a separate channel, and the script keys totals by (type, DT_n jti, key) in a dict the action cannot reach.
- Lab 4: checking an unknown constraint type only when the action names its key would permit actions under (quota, units). Section 4.2 denies every action.
- Lab 4 choices where the -03 is silent (printed at the top of `vectors/full_verify.log` as A1 to A12): (A1) ath is the hash of DT_n's compact serialization, since the -03 does not say which token of a chain is the access token or how the chain is sent. (A2) The DPoP iat window is 60 seconds either way and no nonce is issued. (A3) htu is compared byte for byte after dropping query and fragment, with no RFC 3986 normalization. (A4) A proof jti seen once is refused (RFC 9449 Section 11.1 leaves the replay cache to the server). (A5) The status reference is `"status": {"status_list": {"uri", "idx"}}`, because the -03 names no claim; the cached list is an in-memory model, not a signed status list token. (A6) A cached answer is trusted while now < fetched_at + ttl. (A7) A reference without a usable uri and idx, or an idx outside the list, is status_unknown. (A8) "suspended" is revoked. (A9) A missing or wrongly typed per-action quantity is ceiling_exceeded. (A10) The running total includes A, and a missing total is ceiling_exceeded. (A11) Inside step 8 the order is audience, scope, unknown type, per-action, cumulative, so an unknown type wins over a ceiling. Step 8 says audience "then" scope, per-action and cumulative constraints, and does not place the unknown-type deny of Section 4.2 in that sequence. (A12) Totals are looked up for DT_n's jti only; step 8 checks DT_n's Authority, so an ancestor's max_subtree total is not consulted, which leaves the sibling overspend of Lab 2 to the machinery Section 9.4 describes. (A13) A DPoP proof must use alg "Ed25519" (RFC 9864), the value Section 3 requires for Delegation Tokens; a proof with "EdDSA" (RFC 8037) is denied. The -03 does not say which alg a DPoP proof may use.
